Headline claim: **50% API price cuts** vs. their GPT‑5.6 promotional pricing (Sol: $4→$2 input / $20→$10 output per 1M tokens; Luna: $0.20→$0.10 / $1.20→$0.50). Both inherit Astra's professional-work, factuality, coding, computer-use, and alignment improvements at lower cost, and both get Astra's revised (less jargon-heavy) communication style. Benchmarked heavily against Claude Opus 5 and "Claude Fable 5.1" on AutomationBench, Agents' Last Exam, FrontierCode, DeepSWE, and OSWorld 2.0 — consistent framing throughout is "comparable or better score, meaningfully lower cost per task" versus Anthropic's top models.

Ships a new Prompt Caching Dashboard and a diagnostics tool that reports *why* a cache miss happened (e.g. `tools_changed`). Also newly supports changing reasoning effort mid-conversation without invalidating the cache. Customer quotes from GitHub Copilot (Mario Rodriguez, CPO — "reduced by more than 50% the share of prompt tokens requiring fresh processing"), and agent-infra companies citing hit-rate jumps from ~83–85% to 90%+.
- **[Parallel cut research time and cost in half with GPT‑6 Astra](../../pages/openai.com/index/parallel-cuts-time-and-cost-with-astra/index.md)** — startup customer story. Parallel (AI web-research infra for financial/legal customers) reports GPT‑6 Astra compiled a 6-state/6-statistic labor-market research task in half the time and at roughly half the token cost of prior models, with more targeted search queries and better support for delegating sub-tasks to parallel sub-agents.
- **[Priorities and principles for effective third party assessments](../../pages/openai.com/index/priorities-principles-third-party-assessments/index.md)** — policy/safety essay. Lays out four priority areas OpenAI wants independent assessors to focus on: (1) safety cases spanning training→deployment, (2) safeguards against misuse/loss-of-control, (3) Preparedness Framework capability evaluations (chem/bio, cyber, self-improvement) and alignment evals, and (4) independent investigation of misalignment incidents (explicitly cites the Hugging Face incident as the kind of case this covers). Pairs with seven stated principles (scoped claims, proportionate access, transparent methodology, assessor independence, security/confidentiality, actionable findings, responsible publication). Reads as a companion piece to 2026-09-21's "Building standards for the next phase of AI" — both are part of a visible push this week to formalize OpenAI's public position on external oversight of frontier development.
